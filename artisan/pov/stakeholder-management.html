Stakeholder Management

Getting people across teams aligned and moving in the same direction, even without formal authority over them — built as a repeatable system for an 80+ person team, not just one person's personal habits.

Why This Stays Human Work
AI can draft a status update. It cannot decide what a VP actually needs to hear versus what a teammate needs to hear, or judge when a relationship needs a phone call instead of a message. What it can do is remove the busywork around those judgment calls — so the actual stakeholder-management skill gets more attention, not less.
Automatable
Pulling Context Together
Gathering the facts, drafting a first pass, formatting for the audience
Stays Human
Judging the Relationship
What to say, to whom, when, and whether to send it at all
The Same Facts, Framed for Each Audience
A status-update skill drafts executive, program, and team-level updates from the exact same underlying project signal — recognizing that a VP and a teammate need the same truth presented completely differently, rather than blasting one generic update to everyone and hoping it lands.
Nothing External Sends Itself
Reply-drafting for chat and email pulls real thread context so the draft isn't generic — but every single one is a draft. Nothing goes out without an explicit human review and approval. This is a deliberate design choice to protect the stakeholder relationship, not a limitation of the tooling.
Meeting Prep, Both Directions
A meeting-prep skill builds a sourced briefing pack before a meeting from real calendar, email, and chat context — not a guess at what's relevant — and on the other side, captures decisions and action owners after the meeting ends. "Let me pull together some notes from memory" becomes something structured and traceable instead.
A Repeatable Ceremony Pipeline
The same pipeline runs every quarter across every stakeholder audience it serves — not reinvented each cycle:
Quarterly Review Pipeline
Cycle opens → live status pulled from the system of record → thin or owner-less items auto-flagged before the review → narrative and deck generated from that same verified data → presented
Flagging gaps before the meeting — not during it, in front of stakeholders — is the actual point. One verified source of truth drives both the story and the slides.
Respecting Other Teams' Time
Cross-team handoff docs mean another team — support, sales, docs — gets a structured, accurate handoff instead of needing to schedule a live meeting just to get context. Not scheduling an unnecessary meeting is itself a stakeholder-management discipline, and it scales in a way a live sync never does.
An 80+ person team, five product lines, and one shared set of dashboards, ceremonies, and comms skills — every one of them designed around a real audience's information needs, not a single person's inbox.
